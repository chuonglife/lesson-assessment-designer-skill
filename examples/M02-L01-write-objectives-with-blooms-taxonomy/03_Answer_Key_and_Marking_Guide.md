## 2. Explanations (by source item)

### Q01 · Multiple choice · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

The version of Bloom's taxonomy used in curriculum documents today is a revision by whom, and in which year?

- ✘ Bloom and colleagues, 1956
- **✔** Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001
- ✘ Mager, 1994
- ✘ Dale, 1969

**Explanation**: 1956 is Bloom's original. The version in use is the 2001 revision edited by Anderson and Krathwohl. Mager wrote about behavioural objectives and Dale drew the cone of experience; neither revised the taxonomy.

*Review*: Video 2, section 2.1, slide 6

### Q02 · Multiple choice · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

What is the correct order of the six levels of the 2001 taxonomy, from lowest to highest?

- ✘ Remember, Understand, Analyze, Apply, Evaluate, Create
- **✔** Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create
- ✘ Understand, Remember, Apply, Analyze, Create, Evaluate
- ✘ Remember, Apply, Understand, Analyze, Evaluate, Create

**Explanation**: Apply comes before Analyze: you can do a familiar task before you can take its structure apart. The other options swap Remember and Understand or Evaluate and Create, the two places learners mix up most.

*Review*: Video 2, section 2.1, slide 7

### Q03 · Multiple choice · Understanding · measures K1 · 0.4 points

What is the biggest change in the 2001 taxonomy compared with the 1956 original?

- ✘ Remember was dropped as unnecessary for adults
- ✘ A seventh level was added for emotions
- **✔** Levels became verbs, and Create moved to the top
- ✘ Understand and Apply were merged into one level

**Explanation**: The 2001 revision renamed the levels from nouns (Knowledge) to verbs (Remember) to stress what the learner does, and put Synthesis, renamed Create, at the top. No level was dropped or merged.

*Review*: Video 2, section 2.1, slide 8

### Q04 · Multiple choice · Understanding · measures K1 · 0.4 points

At which Bloom level is the objective "Point out where the questions become chained in a given 8-minute video of a lesson"?

- ✘ Understand
- ✘ Apply
- **✔** Analyze
- ✘ Create

**Explanation**: The learner has to break the lesson into its questions and find the faulty spot, which is Analyze. Understand would only ask what chained questions are; Apply would be writing questions from a model; Create would be designing a new question set.

*Review*: Video 2, section 2.1, pause question

### Q05 · Multiple choice · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

Which verb below should NOT open a learning objective?

- ✘ List
- **✔** Grasp
- ✘ Classify
- ✘ Design

**Explanation**: "Grasp" belongs with understand, know and be aware of: it does not say what the learner will do so you can check it. The other three verbs are on the Bloom verb sheet.

*Review*: Video 3, section 2.2, handout M02-L1-1

### Q06 · Multiple choice · Understanding · measures K1 · 0.4 points

At which level is the objective "Compare two ways of opening a lesson against 3 criteria and choose the one that suits your class"?

- ✘ Understand
- ✘ Analyze
- **✔** Evaluate
- ✘ Create

**Explanation**: There are criteria and there is a choice, so the learner judges value: Evaluate. Comparing without choosing would be Analyze. Many people pick Analyze because they see the word "compare".

*Review*: Video 2, section 2.1, slide 9

### Q07 · Multiple choice · Understanding · measures K1 · 0.4 points

Why is the verb "understand" kept out of the start of an objective?

- ✘ Understanding matters less than doing
- **✔** It does not say how the objective will be checked
- ✘ Adults reach it too easily
- ✘ Curriculum regulations forbid the word

**Explanation**: An action verb is the check written into the objective. "Understand" is a fine word, but the moment you test it you have to swap it for name, explain or sort. No regulation forbids it; regulations ask for measurable verbs.

*Review*: Video 3, section 2.2, slide 10

### Q08 · Multiple choice · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

In step 2 of the objective-table process, at which level should the first lessons of a course usually sit?

- ✘ Levels 5 to 6, to impress learners
- **✔** Levels 1 to 2, rising towards the end
- ✘ Level 3, to match every other lesson
- ✘ Whatever level the instructor likes

**Explanation**: A course climbs: low at the start, high at the end, and the last lesson has a product at level 5 or 6. A course where every lesson sits at the same level is flat; push one or two final lessons up.

*Review*: Video 5, section 3.1, step 2

### Q09 · Multiple choice · Understanding · measures K1 · 0.4 points

All four objectives below use the verb "write". Which one is at the Create level?

- ✘ Write one objective from a given sentence pattern
- **✔** Write the full objective set for a new course of your own
- ✘ Write a model sentence again in your own words
- ✘ Write titles for 5 videos that already exist

**Explanation**: The level comes from what the learner does with knowledge, not from the verb. Writing a whole set for a new course builds a new structure from many parts: Create. From a pattern is Apply; in your own words is Understand; titling existing videos is routine Apply.

*Review*: Video 3, section 2.2, slide 11

### Q10 · Multiple choice · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

How is "Describe" at the start of a knowledge objective in a vocational curriculum treated?

- ✘ As a fault, it has to be replaced
- **✔** As acceptable, at the Understand level
- ✘ As a Create-level verb
- ✘ As one of the banned verbs

**Explanation**: Describe is an action verb at the Understand level, common in vocational programs and checkable orally or in writing. It is nothing like understand, know or grasp.

*Review*: Video 3, section 2.2, handout M02-L1-1

### Q11 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

Hannah, a Year 9 teacher in Leeds, writes this objective for her online course: "Learners will grasp how to ask open questions." Which fault should she fix first?

- ✘ The action has no object
- **✔** The verb cannot be measured
- ✘ There is no condition
- ✘ It is written from the teacher's side

**Explanation**: "Grasp" makes the whole objective uncheckable, so it goes first. The object is there (how to ask open questions) and it is written from the learner's side. The missing condition is the second fix.

*Review*: Video 4, section 2.3; Video 6, checklist criterion 1

### Q12 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

You score "Help learners know how to write objectives" with the 6-criterion checklist. Which result is correct?

- ✘ It passes all 6 criteria
- ✘ Fails criterion 6 only: two tasks in one line
- **✔** Fails criteria 1 and 6: "know" and the teacher's side
- ✘ Fails criterion 4 only: no standard

**Explanation**: "Help" is the teacher's job, not the learner's (criterion 6), and "know" cannot be measured (criterion 1). There is only one task, so it does not fail for two tasks. It also lacks a standard, but the option claiming criterion 4 alone misses both real faults.

*Review*: Video 6, section 3.4, scoring demo table A

### Q13 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

A learner submits the objective "Design effective slides". Which criterion does it fail most clearly?

- ✘ The verb "design" cannot be measured
- **✔** "Effective" cannot be observed or counted
- ✘ Two different tasks are joined with "and"
- ✘ It is written from the instructor's side

**Explanation**: Design is a good action verb. The fault is "effective": you cannot count it or see it, so swap it for a number of slides, lines, colours or a template. The object and condition are thin too, but criterion 4 is the obvious failure.

*Review*: Video 4, section 2.3, slide 14 on the 6 kinds of standard

### Q14 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

Marco teaches office software in Bologna and writes: "Write a video script and record an 8-minute video." What is wrong with it?

- ✘ Nothing, it passes all 6 criteria
- ✘ The verb cannot be measured
- **✔** Two tasks that are checked apart share one line
- ✘ There is no standard for length

**Explanation**: The script is handed in first and the video later, each checked with a different tool, so they need two lines (criterion 6). The verbs and the 8-minute standard are fine.

*Review*: Video 6, section 3.4, scoring demo table B

### Q15 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

How does the objective "List the 6 levels of the 2001 Bloom's taxonomy in the correct order, closed book" score on the 6-criterion checklist?

- **✔** It passes all 6 criteria
- ✘ Fails criterion 3: no condition
- ✘ Fails criterion 4: no standard
- ✘ Fails criterion 5: verb at the wrong level

**Explanation**: Verb list (Remember), object the 6 levels, condition closed book, standard correct order, one task, learner's side. All 6. People who pick a fault usually miss that "closed book" is the condition and "correct order" is the standard.

*Review*: Video 4, section 2.3, slide 13

### Q16 · Multiple choice · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

Priya, a soft-skills trainer in Pune, writes "Design 10 beautiful slides for the lecture". When she scores it herself, which criterion should she mark as failed?

- ✘ Criterion 2, no object
- **✔** Criterion 4, "beautiful" cannot be observed
- ✘ Criterion 1, not an action verb
- ✘ Criterion 6, written from the teacher's side

**Explanation**: The object is there (10 slides), the verb is there (design), the side is right. "Beautiful" is a judgement, so replace it: one idea per slide, at most 6 lines, at most 3 colours, on the slide master made earlier.

*Review*: Video 6, section 3.4, pause question

### Q17 · True or false · Understanding · measures K2 · 0.4 points

The objective "Turn 10 given closed questions into open questions, 10/10 of them impossible to answer with yes or no" already has a countable standard.

Answer: **TRUE**

**Explanation**: 10/10 together with the sign "impossible to answer with yes or no" is a standard of the number-plus-observable-quality kind.

*Review*: Video 5, section 3.2, demonstration of step 3

### Q18 · True or false · Understanding · measures K2 · 0.4 points

The line "Bloom's taxonomy 2001" passes as an objective because it names the lesson content correctly.

Answer: **FALSE**

**Explanation**: That is a content heading, not an objective: no verb, nothing the learner does. It is fault number 1 in the table of common mistakes.

*Review*: Video 5, section 3.2, common mistakes table

### Q20 · True or false · Understanding · measures K2 · 0.4 points

An objective table for a Udemy course should end up with 3 to 5 course-level objectives, each tied to a real product the learner submits.

Answer: **TRUE**

**Explanation**: Step 4 of the process: gather 3 to 5 course-level objectives from the largest products; write the check first, then the objective. A table with 8 to 11 lines is the greedy-table mistake.

*Review*: Video 5, section 3.1, step 4; Video 6 close

### Q19 · Matching · Routine application · measures K2 · 0.4 points

Match each objective on the left with the correct scoring result on the right.

Answer: Be aware of the importance of objectives → Fails 1 and 4: unmeasurable verb, no standard; Write 3 course-level objectives on the template, each with all 4 components → Passes all 6 criteria; Evaluate a colleague's objective table → Fails 3 and 4: no scoring tool, no result to count

**Explanation**: Line 1 uses "be aware of" and has no standard. Line 2 has verb, object, condition (the template) and standard (all 4 components). Line 3 has a good verb but says neither what it is scored with nor what the result looks like; rewrite as "using the 6-criterion checklist, naming the failed criterion of each line".

*Review*: Video 6, section 3.4, the 6-criterion checklist

### Q21 · Fill in the blank · Recall · measures K1 · 0.4 points

A complete learning objective has four components: an action verb, an object, a ____ and a ____.

- Blank 1: **condition** (accept: condition, conditions, condition or tool, context)
- Blank 2: **standard** (accept: standard, criterion, measurable standard, performance standard)

**Explanation**: The formula is verb + object + condition, tool or context + standard. The two blanks may come in either order.

*Review*: Video 4, section 2.3, slide 13

### Q22 · Short answer · Higher-order application · measures S1 · 1.6 points

Tom teaches air-conditioning repair at a college in Cardiff and is building his first Udemy course. His lesson objective reads: "Help learners understand the refrigerant charging procedure." Rewrite it as one objective with all 4 components, at the Apply level.

| Point to look for | points |
|---|---|
| A measurable action verb at the Apply level (carry out, charge, complete); no "help" or "understand" left | 0.4 |
| A concrete object: the charging procedure for a named unit, a number of steps or the procedure's name | 0.4 |
| A condition or tool: on which training rig, with a manifold gauge set, following the procedure sheet | 0.4 |
| A countable standard: correct order of N steps, pressure inside a given range, a time limit, no safety breach | 0.4 |

*Model answer that passes*: Carry out the 7-step R32 charging procedure on a 9,000 BTU wall-mounted training unit, using a manifold gauge set and the procedure sheet, in the correct order, with suction pressure between 110 and 130 psi, within 30 minutes and with no safety breach.

**Explanation**: 0.4 per point; give 0.2 when the point is there but vague (for example "correct technique" with no number).

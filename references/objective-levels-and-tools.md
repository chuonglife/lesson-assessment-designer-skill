# Objective levels, verbs and the assessment tools that fit

Source: *Summary table of objectives and the verbs to use* (Anderson, Krathwohl et al. 2001; Pohl 2000; Dave 1975; Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia 1973), Module 06, Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology and Education. Use it to (1) read the verb of each objective and give it a blueprint level, and (2) pick the item type or tool for that level.

## 1. Cognitive domain (knowledge), revised Bloom 2001

| # | Level | What it is | Typical verbs | Assessment methods |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Remember** | Recall facts, dates, events, main ideas | state, list, define, describe, name, identify, quote, recognise, copy, arrange | short questions, selected-response, give an example, define a term, list |
| 2 | **Understand** | Grasp meaning; put it another way; classify, restructure; explain in a situation; predict consequences | describe, summarise, explain, interpret, predict, distinguish, compare, estimate, rewrite, translate | selected-response, short written answers, simple comparisons, summaries, outlines, reports, discussion, one-minute paper, muddiest point |
| 3 | **Apply** | Use a method, concept or theory in a new situation; solve practical problems | apply, use, demonstrate, calculate, complete, solve, check, adjust, change, classify, operate, predict | illustration, presentation, project, role play, problem solving, simulation, practical work, forecasting, charting |
| 4 | **Analyse** | Break material into parts to see its structure; tell facts from inferences | analyse, compare, contrast, diagram, decode, distinguish, identify, outline, relate, select, separate | case studies, comparisons, deduction, discussion, concept maps, problem cases, questions, reports |
| 5 | **Evaluate** | Judge the value of ideas or material | appraise, judge, conclude, critique, defend, evaluate, justify, support, argue | debate, case studies, defending a position, group discussion, project, self- and peer assessment, written conclusions |
| 6 | **Create** | Build a new structure or model from parts; combine parts into a new whole | combine, compose, draft, create, develop, design, modify, organise, plan, rearrange, rebuild, rewrite | new concepts, creative application, experiments, data collection, redesign, projects, problem solving, simulation |

Several verbs appear at more than one level (describe, compare, distinguish, explain). The level comes from the **object and the standard**: "describe the 6 Bloom levels in order" is Remember; "describe why the 2001 revision turned nouns into verbs" is Understand; "compare two given objectives and say which one can be measured" is Analyse.

## 2. Psychomotor domain (skills), Dave 1975

| # | Level | What it is | Verbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Imitation** | Watch and copy; quality may be low | copy, follow, imitate, repeat, replicate |
| 2 | **Manipulation** | Perform from memory or from instructions | act, perform, carry out |
| 3 | **Precision** | Perform accurately without help; could demonstrate to a beginner | calibrate, demonstrate, perfect |
| 4 | **Articulation** | Combine and adapt several actions into a consistent whole | adapt, build, create, modify |
| 5 | **Naturalisation** | Perform fluently, fast and accurately without thinking | design, develop a product |

Tools: observe performance with a **checklist**; score products with a **criteria sheet** or a **rubric**; at levels 4 and 5 add varied situations and time limits.

## 3. Affective domain (attitudes), Krathwohl, Bloom and Masia 1973

| # | Level | What it is | Verbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Receiving** | Awareness, willingness to listen, selective attention | ask, choose, describe, follow, hold, identify, name, point to, reply, use |
| 2 | **Responding** | Active participation, reacting, willingness to answer | answer, assist, comply, discuss, help, perform, practise, read, report, tell, write |
| 3 | **Valuing** | Attaching worth to an object or behaviour, from acceptance to commitment | complete, demonstrate, differentiate, explain, invite, join, justify, propose, share |
| 4 | **Organisation** | Ranking values, resolving conflicts between them | adhere, alter, arrange, combine, compare, defend, integrate, modify, organise, prepare, synthesise |
| 5 | **Characterisation** | A value system that steers behaviour consistently | act, display, influence, listen, practise, propose, solve, verify |

Common verbs in attitude objectives: comply with, support, oppose, take part in, accept, defend, cooperate, take care to, take initiative, take responsibility for.

Tools: observe behaviour while learners do the assignment (rubric rows such as on time, revised after feedback, original work), self-assessment, conversation. **Do not measure attitudes with knowledge items**: "Should a teacher...?" checks whether they know the right thing, not whether they do it.

## 4. From verb to blueprint level to item type

The blueprint has four columns. The six Bloom levels map onto them like this:

| Blueprint level (`level`) | Bloom levels | What the learner does in the item | Good item types | Poor item types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `recall` | Remember | Recall a name, definition, order, number, step | multiple choice ("what is", "how many", "the third step is"), fill in the blank, matching name to definition, true or false | essay (wastes time) |
| `understand` | Understand | Explain why, tell two close things apart, pick the right example, summarise | multiple choice ("why", "which example", "which statement is right"), matching examples to concepts, true or false with one subtle false element | fill in the blank (only checks memory) |
| `apply` | Apply (familiar situation, like the ones taught) | Use a rule or procedure in a given case; calculate; fix a given fault; classify a case | scenario multiple choice ("Hannah writes objective X; what is wrong?"), matching cases to actions, short answer (rewrite, calculate) | true or false, fill in the blank |
| `higher-order` | Analyse, Evaluate, Create | Handle a new case with several factors; choose the best option with reasons; produce something | short answer with criteria; **an assignment with a rubric**; complex scenario multiple choice ("the best next step") only checks recognising, not producing | true or false, fill in the blank, matching |

Two rules when you set an item's level: **never above the objective's level** (learners fail an objective they met) and **not below it** (learners pass one they did not). Measuring a higher-order objective with recall items is the most common validity fault; the script blocks an item two levels below its objective and warns at one level below an application objective.

## 5. Skill and attitude objectives in a lesson's assessment

| Kind of objective | Example | How to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Skill: make a product | "Write at least 3 objectives for your own course, each with all 4 components" | Submitted assignment with an analytic rubric or a checklist per objective; items only for the knowledge behind it ("which component is missing?") |
| Skill: a procedure | "Set up an external microphone so a 30-second test recording has no hiss" | Submitted recording plus a procedure checklist (screenshot of the settings) |
| Skill: self-checking | "Score your own objective table with the 6-criterion checklist" | Submitted completed checklist; rubric row "lines where your score matches the instructor's" |
| Attitude / autonomy | "Remove every unmeasurable verb; revise on feedback within 7 days" | Rubric rows in the assignment: zero banned verbs; revision submitted on time |

If the objectives file has an alignment table (objective → section → how it is checked), start from its last column. The test must agree with it. If the table is wrong (for example a skill checked by a quiz), fix the objectives file first and say so.

# Worked example: M02-L01, Write learning objectives with Bloom's taxonomy

The full pack is in `examples/M02-L01-write-objectives-with-blooms-taxonomy/`; its `assessment.json` is also `templates/assessment.example.json`. Read this to calibrate **difficulty, voice and explanation length**, not to copy items.

## The brief

The lesson's objectives file (from the Lesson Content Writer example of the same name) has five objectives: K1 (the 6 Bloom levels with verbs, standard 8/10), K2 (tell right from wrong in 10 objectives and name the fault, 8/10), S1 (write the objective table for your own course), S2 (self-score with the 6-criterion checklist), A1 (no banned verbs, revise within 7 days). A 150-minute self-paced video lesson with 2 quizzes and 1 submitted assignment, for teachers and trainers building their first online course.

## The decisions, step by step

| Step | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Purpose | `summative`, the two quizzes merged into one 25-minute end-of-lesson test | Both quizzes sit at the end of the theory part; one test shows the whole blueprint. On Udemy, Part A (mostly K1) and Parts B to D (K2) are still split into two quizzes |
| 2 Objectives | K1 → test, 11 items; K2 → test, 10 items; S1 → `both` (one short answer + the assignment); S2 and A1 → assignment only | The 8/10 standard needs at least 10 items per objective; writing a table cannot be measured by selected response |
| 3 Blueprint | 3 topics by video group: Bloom 35%, formula and checklist 55%, process 10%; levels 24 / 32 / 28 / 16 | Topic 2 has most teaching time and underpins the assignment; the 5-step process is measured mostly by the assignment, so it gets 2 items |
| 4 Items | 16 multiple choice, 3 true or false, 1 matching (3 to 6), 1 fill in the blank, 1 short answer | Distractors come from the lesson's common mistakes table and the two scoring demonstrations in Video 6 |
| 5 Points | 21 selected-response items x 0.4 = 8.4; short answer 1.6; total 10; pass 8 | The objectives say 8/10; 80% is common on Udemy |
| 6 Review | Script: 0 blocking errors, 3 warnings kept | 3 true or false items at understanding level measure an application objective: kept, because they carry only 1.2 points and 7 application items already cover K2 |

## Three items to calibrate the voice

**Recall, distractors from real verbs** (Q05): "Which verb below should NOT open a learning objective? A. List · B. Grasp · C. Classify · D. Design." The negative is in capitals; the three distractors are valid verbs from the lesson's verb sheet.

**Routine application, a person and a place** (Q14): "Marco teaches office software in Bologna and writes: 'Write a video script and record an 8-minute video.' What is wrong with it?" Key: two tasks that are checked apart share one line. The explanation goes straight to the confusion: "The script is handed in first and the video later, each checked with a different tool, so they need two lines. The verbs and the 8-minute standard are fine."

**Short answer with its criteria in the question** (Q22): Tom, who teaches air-conditioning repair, wrote "Help learners understand the refrigerant charging procedure"; rewrite it with all 4 components at the Apply level; 4 points x 0.4, "scored on the 4 points below, not on air-conditioning knowledge"; a model answer that passes is in the key.

## The assignment and rubric

A-1 puts S1, S2 and A1 into one product (the objective table plus the completed checklist), marked with an analytic rubric of 4 criteria x 4 levels worth 3 / 3 / 2 / 2. Every descriptor has a number: "more than 2 lines still use a banned verb", "50 to 80% of lines have all 4 components", "no more than 1 line differs from the instructor's scoring". The attitude objective A1 lives in criterion 1 (zero banned verbs) and criterion 4 (on time, revision within 7 days). Pass: at least 6.5 with no criterion at level 1.

## Where people go wrong copying this

- Writing recall items for a higher-order objective and calling it measured. "Write" is only measured once there is a product in an assignment.
- Letting the key be the longest option. In the first draft, Q03 and Q13 were flagged by the script; the fix was to trim the key and lengthen the distractors, not to ignore the warning.
- Padding to four options with distractors nobody would choose. If the lesson only supports three plausible options, switch the whole test to 3 options; do not invent a silly fourth.
- Leaving out `source`, so the "after marking" list cannot point to the video that needs fixing.
